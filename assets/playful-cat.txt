7. Six-eye fish tracking

All six eyes must track the custom blue fish pointer anywhere within the supported desktop viewport.

For each eye:

- Record its screen-space or artwork-space center.
- Calculate the vector from the eye center to the fish.
- Normalize the vector.
- Clamp movement to an eye-specific ellipse.
- Move only the iris-and-pupil layer within the mask.
- Smooth movement with requestAnimationFrame.

Do not force the pupils to reach the exact fish coordinate when the fish is at a far screen edge. Natural gaze is more important than exact geometric alignment.

Use these behaviors:

- Clamp every eye at a comfortable maximum before it reaches the eyelid edge.
- Add a small dead zone to prevent jitter near the neutral target.
- Use a distance factor that stops increasing after a practical range.
- Keep all irises fully inside their eye masks.
- Prevent crossed eyes and mismatched directions.
- Keep the gaze active while the fish is over the lock or Token input.

The three cats may use subtly different gaze easing so they do not look mechanically synchronized:

- Brown tabby: approximately 90–125ms perceived response.
- Orange-and-white cat: approximately 110–145ms.
- White cat: approximately 125–165ms.

When the pointer leaves the viewport, ease all eyes back toward the upper-left or upper-center neutral point over approximately 350–500ms.

Do not rotate or warp the whole head in the first implementation. A head tilt may be considered later, but it is not required for convincing gaze tracking and must not be included if it reduces likeness.

8. Independent tail animation

The movement should be subtle and cat-like:

- Slow side-to-side sway.
- Small rotation arc.
- Gentle ease-in-out timing.
- No whipping, wagging like a dog, bouncing, spiraling, or identical synchronized motion.
- The tail must remain visually connected to the body at the base.
- The moving layer must not reveal a hole or cut edge in the body artwork.
- Avoid moving the entire body.

Suggested initial motion values:

- Brown tabby tail: 4.8s duration, approximately ±5 degrees, phase offset 0s.
- Orange-and-white tail: 6.4s duration, approximately ±7 degrees, phase offset -1.7s.
- White cat tail: 5.5s duration, approximately ±4 degrees, phase offset -3.1s.

These values are starting points and should be tuned after visual testing. The exact movements must feel different but belong to the same calm scene.

Use CSS keyframes or a small requestAnimationFrame loop. Prefer CSS animations if the prepared tail pivots allow clean rotation. Use `transform-origin` at the anatomical tail base and animate only `transform`.

Optional refinement:

- Add a second very small translate or bend-like component only if the single-pivot rotation looks rigid.
- Do not add a full skeletal animation system for the first version.

When `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active:

- Stop all continuous tail animation.
- Keep tails in their neutral positions.
- Remove decorative reveal and hover movement.
- Eye tracking may update directly with reduced or no smoothing because it communicates pointer position.
